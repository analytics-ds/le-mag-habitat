Setting up a terrace does not necessarily require a large budget. A few well-thought-out choices are enough to transform an outdoor space.

## In brief

1. Prioritise items based on available budget rather than doing everything at once.
2. Bet on potted plants to green up the space without construction work.
3. Choose second-hand or modular furniture.
4. Add simple lighting to extend use into the evening.
5. Save costly flooring for a second phase, if needed.

## Prioritising the design items

Rather than funding everything at once, it is more realistic to prioritise: furniture and planting first, then possibly flooring or a pergola later, once budget becomes available.

## Greening the space without construction work using potted plants

| Plant type | Advantage |
|---|---|
| Perennials | Low maintenance, return every year |
| Climbing plants | Create vertical privacy |
| Aromatic herbs | Useful day to day, low cost |

Potted plants allow a terrace to be greened up quickly, with no structural work, and can be moved around according to the seasons or preferences.

## Betting on second-hand or modular furniture

Second-hand furniture, available on peer-to-peer sales platforms, often allows furnishing a terrace at a lower cost. Modular or foldable furniture is also a practical option for small outdoor spaces.

## Simple lighting to extend evenings

String lights, solar lamps or candle holders are enough to create a pleasant atmosphere in the evening, with no need for complex electrical installation.

## Thinking of the outdoors as an extension of the home

This small-budget design approach ties in with the principles applied indoors to optimise [storage in a small space](/en/blog/small-space-storage-tips/), with the same goal: making the most of every available square metre.

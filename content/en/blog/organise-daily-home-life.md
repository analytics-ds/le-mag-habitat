Organising everyday home life does not require major upheaval, just a few simple habits introduced gradually.

## In brief

1. Spread household chores across the week rather than grouping them all together.
2. Devote a short but regular amount of time each day to everyday upkeep.
3. Declutter regularly to reduce tidying time.
4. Adapt the organisation to the household's actual constraints.
5. Involve everyone in the household in sharing out the tasks.
6. Formalise a cleaning schedule to visualise the sharing of tasks.

## Spreading rather than concentrating

Grouping all household chores into a single day can quickly become discouraging. Spreading cleaning, tidying and maintenance across several days of the week keeps a sustainable pace, with shorter, less tedious sessions.

## Devoting a short but regular amount of time

| Frequency | Type of task |
|---|---|
| Daily | Dishes, quick tidying, airing rooms |
| Weekly | Floor cleaning, bathroom, dusting |
| Monthly | Deep cleaning (oven, windows, textiles) |

This spread-out approach avoids build-up and makes each session quicker, since tasks do not pile up from one week to the next.

## Building a cleaning schedule suited to your family

A schedule, displayed in the kitchen or shared through a family app, helps visualise who does what and when. For a family with children, it helps to assign simple tasks suited to each person's age (putting away toys, setting the table), rather than leaving everything to a single adult in the household. The key is that the schedule stays visible to everyone and adjustable, since an overly rigid organisation is often abandoned after a few weeks.

## Decluttering to save time

A less cluttered home requires less tidying time day to day. The principles applied to [optimise storage in a small space](/en/blog/small-space-storage-tips/) apply just as well in a larger home, with the same benefit on time spent on maintenance.

## Adapting the organisation to your pace of life

There is no universal method: the most effective organisation remains the one that adapts to the household's actual constraints (work schedules, children, home size), rather than a theoretical schedule that is hard to stick to over time.

## Sharing the load fairly among household members

Regularly rotating the least popular tasks, rather than always assigning them to the same person, prevents the mental load of running the household from falling on a single member. A quick weekly family check-in also helps adjust the sharing of tasks if schedules change.

## An organisation that extends to technical upkeep

This organisational logic also applies to less visible tasks, such as the [natural cleaning of kitchen equipment](/en/blog/clean-oven-without-chemicals/) or regularly checking the home's installations, as well as outdoor upkeep when the home has a [terrace set up on a budget](/en/blog/small-budget-terrace-design/).

Facing a broken appliance, the question always comes up: repair or replace? A simple method helps decide without relying on gut feeling alone.

## In brief

1. Compare the repair cost with the price of an equivalent new appliance.
2. Take into account the appliance's age and average lifespan.
3. Ask for a fault diagnosis before deciding.
4. Also consider the availability of spare parts.
5. Do not overlook the refurbished option as an alternative to new.

## The 50% rule

A simple and widely used rule is to compare the repair quote with the price of an equivalent new appliance. If the repair costs more than half the price of a new one, replacement generally becomes the more rational choice, except for a recent or high-end appliance.

## The appliance's age, a decisive factor

| Appliance | Average lifespan |
|---|---|
| Washing machine | 8 to 10 years |
| Refrigerator | 10 to 15 years |
| Dishwasher | 9 to 12 years |
| Oven | 12 to 15 years |

An appliance close to its theoretical end of life more easily justifies a replacement, even for a seemingly minor fault, as other components risk failing shortly after.

## The importance of a fault diagnosis

Before any decision, a precise fault diagnosis remains essential. It identifies the faulty part, estimates the actual repair cost, and avoids replacing an appliance for a fault that turns out to be minor and inexpensive to fix.

## Refurbished, an alternative between repair and new

When repair is no longer relevant, replacement does not necessarily mean buying new. Choosing an appliance such as a [refurbished refrigerator](/en/blog/buy-refurbished-refrigerator/) combines savings with sustainability.

## Anticipating rather than enduring

Regular maintenance of household appliances helps spot signs of wear before a full breakdown, and better anticipate the budget to plan for. This vigilance is part of a broader approach to managing the home's [consumption](/en/blog/reduce-electricity-bill/).

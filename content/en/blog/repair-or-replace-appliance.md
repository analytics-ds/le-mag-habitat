Facing a broken appliance, the question always comes up: repair or replace? A simple method helps decide without relying on gut feeling alone.

## In brief

1. Compare the repair cost with the price of an equivalent new appliance.
2. Take into account the appliance's age and average lifespan.
3. Ask for a fault diagnosis before deciding.
4. Also consider the availability of spare parts.
5. Check whether a repair subsidy applies to the breakdown in question.
6. Do not overlook the refurbished option as an alternative to new.

## The 50% rule

A simple and widely used rule is to compare the repair quote with the price of an equivalent new appliance. If the repair costs more than half the price of a new one, replacement generally becomes the more rational choice, except for a recent or high-end appliance.

This rule remains a decision baseline, not an absolute law: an appliance still under manufacturer warranty, or with a minor fault (belt, seal, thermostat), can justify a repair even close to this threshold, if it clearly extends the appliance's lifespan.

## The appliance's age, a decisive factor

| Appliance | Average lifespan |
|---|---|
| Washing machine | 8 to 10 years |
| Refrigerator | 10 to 15 years |
| Dishwasher | 9 to 12 years |
| Oven | 12 to 15 years |

An appliance close to its theoretical end of life more easily justifies a replacement, even for a seemingly minor fault, as other components risk failing shortly after. Conversely, an appliance under 5 years old almost always deserves to be repaired, as the fault is often isolated on a still-recent model.

## The importance of a fault diagnosis

Before any decision, a precise fault diagnosis remains essential. It identifies the faulty part, estimates the actual repair cost, and avoids replacing an appliance for a fault that turns out to be minor and inexpensive to fix. The availability of spare parts also comes into play: some older models or less common brands no longer have parts available, which makes repair impossible even when it would be economically justified.

## Where to get an appliance repaired

Several options exist depending on the complexity of the fault and the available budget. Community repair cafés, volunteer-run workshops found in many towns, let you get an appliance fixed for free with the help of volunteers, for faults that are simple to diagnose. For a more technical fault, a certified professional repairer may give access to a government repair subsidy where one exists, a financial aid that directly reduces the invoice amount, with no paperwork required since it is deducted automatically by the repairer.

The manufacturer's after-sales service remains an option for appliances still under warranty, but often proves more expensive than an independent repairer once the warranty has expired.

## Refurbished, an alternative between repair and new

When repair is no longer relevant, replacement does not necessarily mean buying new. Choosing a refurbished appliance combines savings with sustainability, as long as it comes from a reputable platform offering a warranty.

## Anticipating rather than enduring

Regular maintenance of household appliances helps spot signs of wear before a full breakdown, and better anticipate the budget to plan for. This vigilance is part of a broader approach to managing the home's [consumption](/en/blog/reduce-electricity-bill/) and [everyday organisation](/en/blog/organise-daily-home-life/), since an ageing appliance often also consumes more before breaking down completely. Keeping an eye on your [electricity bill](/en/blog/read-electricity-bill/) is actually a good indirect indicator of how much large appliances in the home are ageing.

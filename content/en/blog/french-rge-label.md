The RGE label comes up in most energy renovation projects in France, whether the work involves insulation, heating, or renewable energy. The acronym stands for Reconnu Garant de l'Environnement, and it certifies building professionals who are qualified for this type of work while also determining access to nearly every financial aid scheme for renovation. For a household preparing a renovation project, understanding exactly what this certification covers helps avoid unpleasant surprises when putting together a funding application. Here is what the label means, which projects it covers, and how to check that a professional actually holds it.

## What the RGE label is

The RGE label is a quality mark awarded to building companies and tradespeople who specialize in energy renovation and renewable energy installations. It was created in 2011 by French public authorities to structure the building trade and ensure the reliability of services offered to homeowners.

The certification confirms that the company applies the energy performance standards relevant to this type of work and has the skills needed to carry it out properly. It is granted after several steps: specialized training for the professional, compliance with technical criteria specific to their line of work, an initial audit, and then regular checks for as long as the qualification remains valid.

The RGE mention is not a single, generic label: it comes in several distinct qualifications depending on the activity carried out. A single company can hold several mentions if it works across different types of projects, such as insulation and heating installation, but each mention requires its own training and its own checks. There is no single all-purpose RGE label covering every building trade.

In practice, a professional certified RGE displays this mention on quotes, invoices, and commercial communication. A homeowner should be able to verify this easily before signing any contract, since it determines whether the project is eligible for financial aid.

## Why the RGE label determines access to financial aid

For several years now, nearly every scheme supporting energy renovation has required hiring an RGE-certified professional in order to qualify. This applies to MaPrimeRenov, energy savings certificates (CEE), the zero-interest eco loan, and the reduced VAT rate that applies to energy renovation work.

This requirement serves a simple purpose: making sure that work funded by public money or by energy suppliers meets real performance criteria, not just a stated intention to save energy. A project carried out by a company that is not certified for the relevant mention will not qualify for any of these aid schemes, regardless of how good the actual work turns out to be.

For a household planning a project such as [attic insulation](/en/blog/attic-insulation-where-to-start/), checking the RGE certification of the company being considered is a step to handle before signing any final quote, on the same footing as timelines or price. Doing this check avoids discovering the aid ineligibility after the work has already started, a situation that can undermine an entire funding plan, including one that relied in part on an [energy voucher](/en/blog/france-energy-voucher/) or another one-off aid to complete the budget.

Combining several aid schemes on the same project, for example MaPrimeRenov together with CEE, generally rests on this same condition of hiring a certified professional: a single missing RGE mention on one part of the work can therefore put the entire funding plan at risk, not only the aid directly tied to it.

## Which projects are covered by RGE certification

The RGE mention does not cover every type of building work: it applies specifically to projects that improve a home's energy performance or install equipment using a renewable energy source. The table below summarizes the main categories of work covered and their corresponding RGE mentions.

| Type of work | Associated RGE mention |
|---|---|
| Thermal insulation (attic, walls, floors) | RGE Insulation |
| External joinery (windows, doors) | RGE Joinery |
| Wood and biomass heating | Qualibois |
| Heat pumps | QualiPAC |
| Solar panels and solar water heating | QualiPV / Quali'ENR |
| Ventilation (simple and dual flow) | RGE Ventilation |
| Energy audit and renovation advice | RGE Studies |

Work that does not touch a home's energy performance, such as a purely cosmetic bathroom makeover or a routine plumbing repair, falls outside the RGE scope and is not subject to this certification requirement. A project combining several trades, such as a full renovation that includes both insulation and a new heating system, may require hiring several companies, each holding the mention that matches its part of the work.

## What happens when hiring a company without RGE certification

Having renovation work covered by the RGE scope carried out by a company that does not hold the certification is not against the law: nothing prevents a homeowner from signing a quote with a non-certified professional. The consequence is purely financial, since the aid application will automatically be rejected by the reviewing body, whether that is the national housing agency, an energy supplier under the CEE scheme, or a bank handling the zero-interest eco loan.

This risk also applies to quotes signed partway through a project: if a company's RGE qualification is suspended or withdrawn between the time the quote is signed and the time the work is carried out, the homeowner loses access to the related aid, even if the original quote mentioned the certification. Asking for recent proof of certification, rather than relying on an older commercial document, limits this risk.

## How to check that a professional is RGE certified

Checking a professional's RGE certification takes only a few minutes and should happen before signing any quote. The official France Renov directory lists every certified company and tradesperson, sorted by RGE mention and geographic area, and allows a direct search by company name or line of work.

It is worth checking two separate things during this process: that the company is indeed certified, and that the specific RGE mention it holds matches the type of work planned. A company certified RGE Insulation is not automatically qualified QualiPAC for installing a heat pump, since the two mentions rest on separate training and separate checks.

The certification number shown on the quote also makes it possible to confirm the information directly with the relevant certifying body, in addition to a search in the online directory. For insulation work funded through energy savings certificates, a technical site visit is generally also required before work begins, which is a further sign of a professional handling the process properly.

A detailed, itemized quote that mentions the RGE qualification, the name of the certifying body, and the certification number remains the key reference document if the aid application is later reviewed. Keeping that quote along with the final invoice and proof of payment makes things easier if the body reviewing the aid application later asks for supporting documents.

## Which bodies issue RGE certification

RGE certification is not issued by a single body: several qualification bodies operate depending on the professional's line of work. Qualibat covers a wide range of building trades, Quali'ENR specializes in renewable energy with its QualiPAC and QualiPV offshoots, and other bodies such as Qualifelec cover more specific trades such as electrical work.

These qualification bodies are themselves accredited to issue the RGE mention within a framework set by public authorities, and trade federations such as CAPEB support their member tradespeople through the certification process. Choosing a body therefore depends mainly on the professional's line of work, not on a homeowner's preference: the same renovation project can involve a company certified by a different body than the one that handled a previous project in the same home.

This structure with several bodies explains why two companies working on the same full renovation project, for example one handling insulation and another handling heating, can display different certification logos on their respective quotes. That does not mean one company is less reliable than the other: each body simply applies a technical framework specific to its own area of expertise, validated by public authorities before being allowed to issue the RGE mention.

## Checks and monitoring: how quality is maintained after certification

Obtaining the RGE label does not stop at initial training followed by a one-off audit. Certifying bodies carry out regular monitoring of qualified companies, in the form of checks on projects already completed, to confirm that the invoiced work actually matches accepted trade standards and the stated energy performance criteria.

This monitoring system can lead to a qualification being withdrawn if a shortfall is found, which prevents the company concerned from letting its clients access financial aid until the situation is corrected. For homeowners, this follow-up mechanism is an extra safeguard: the RGE certification shown at the time of the quote keeps being monitored throughout the working relationship, not just verified once when it was first obtained.

For an energy renovation project, this ongoing oversight translates into better assurance that the completed work will actually lower the [electricity bill](/en/blog/reduce-electricity-bill/) or heating costs over time, not just on paper in the funding application. An unfavorable check carried out after a project is finished does not retroactively cancel aid already paid out, but it can affect access to aid for future projects carried out by the same company.
